Why Your Resume Gets Rejected by ATS (and How to Fix It)

Updated 25 October 2026 · 7 min read

You applied to 40 jobs on Naukri and LinkedIn and heard back from two. Your experience is solid, so what went wrong? Very often the problem is not you — it's how your resume reads to the software that screens it before any recruiter does.

This guide explains what an Applicant Tracking System (ATS) actually does, the most common reasons resumes score poorly, and a simple checklist to fix yours before your next application.

What is an ATS?

An Applicant Tracking System is the software companies use to collect and organise job applications. Most mid-size and large employers in India use one — Workday, SuccessFactors, Greenhouse, Lever, Zoho Recruit, Darwinbox and others.

When you apply, the ATS turns your resume into plain text, splits it into fields (name, experience, skills, education) and lets recruiters search and rank candidates. Recruiters typically search for the skills and job titles in the job description. If your resume doesn't contain those words — or the ATS can't read them — you won't show up near the top.

A common myth: "ATS automatically rejects 75% of resumes." Most systems don't auto-reject on their own. What usually happens is quieter: your resume ranks low in a recruiter's search, so it is never opened. The fix is the same — make your resume easy to parse and clearly matched to the role.

7 reasons your resume scores low

1. Missing keywords from the job description

This is the biggest one. If the JD asks for "Selenium, Java, TestNG, CI/CD, Jenkins" and your resume says "automation framework development", a recruiter searching for Jenkins won't find you. Use the exact terms the employer uses, as long as they are genuinely true for you.

2. Fancy layouts, tables and columns

Two-column templates, text boxes, icons and tables look great to humans but often get scrambled when the ATS converts your file to text. Skills in a sidebar can end up merged with your job history — or dropped entirely. A clean single-column layout is safest.

3. Important text inside images or headers

Contact details placed in the page header/footer, or skills shown as logos and progress bars, are frequently not read at all. Keep your name, phone and email in the main body as normal text.

4. Unusual section headings

"My Journey" or "Where I've Been" is creative, but an ATS looks for standard labels: Summary, Experience, Skills, Education, Projects, Certifications. Stick to these.

5. Job titles that don't match the role

If your company calls you "Member of Technical Staff – II" but the role you want is "Senior QA Engineer", recruiters searching for the second title may miss you. You can write it as Member of Technical Staff – II (Senior QA Engineer) so both humans and software understand.

6. Scanned or image-based PDFs

If your PDF was made by scanning or photographing a printed page, there is no selectable text inside — the ATS sees an empty document. Always export your resume directly from Word or Google Docs.

7. One resume for every job

Sending the same resume to a backend developer role and a DevOps role means it is a weak match for both. Small tweaks per application — summary, top skills, the order of bullets — make a big difference to your match score.

Quick fix checklist

  • Single-column layout, standard fonts (Calibri, Arial, Inter), no tables for main content
  • Contact details in the body, not the header
  • Standard section headings: Summary, Experience, Skills, Education
  • The JD's key skills and tools appear in your Skills section and in your experience bullets
  • Your target job title appears in your summary
  • Bullets start with an action verb and include a result ("Cut regression time from 6 hours to 90 minutes by…")
  • Saved as a text-based PDF or DOCX — try selecting the text; if you can't, the ATS can't either
  • File name like Firstname-Lastname-Resume.pdf

Freshers: what to do when you lack experience

If you're a student or recent graduate, your projects, internships and certifications do the job that work experience does for others. Describe each project using the same technical words a job description uses: the language, framework, database and tools you used, plus what the project achieved. Placement-season JDs are often very similar across companies, so one well-matched resume can be reused with small edits.

Key takeaways

  • An ATS ranks resumes by how well they match a recruiter's search — mostly the skills and titles in the job description.
  • Simple formatting beats fancy templates.
  • Tailor your top skills and summary for every application.
  • Only add keywords that are true for you — interviews will test them.
